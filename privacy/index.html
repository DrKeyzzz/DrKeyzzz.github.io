Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 4, 2026

Music Theory Academy keeps the least student information it can: a first name, a last initial, a class code, the student's results in the games and a simple record of visits. It has no ads, no outside analytics or tracking companies, sends nothing to AI services, and never sells or shares student information.

Who we are

Music Theory Academy is a free set of music theory and ear training games with assignments, quizzes and a teacher dashboard. It was built and is run by David Milnes, a music teacher, for students in music classes. The site is the same at all three of its addresses: musictheoryacademy.org (main), musictheorygames.org, and drkeyzzz.github.io (the original address, which now forwards to the others).

Questions about this policy: privacy@musictheoryacademy.org.

What we collect

From students. Students do not make an account. To save their work they type:

  • a first name
  • a last initial
  • a class code their teacher gives them

While they play, the site saves their results under that name and class code: scores, practice time, level progress, and assignment and quiz results. If a student uses the optional feedback box, their short note (up to 300 characters) is saved too.

Visit log. When someone has a game open for at least 20 seconds, the site saves one short record of that visit: the first name and last initial (or "Guest"), the class code, which game and which part of it, the kind of device (for example "Phone · iPhone · Safari" and the screen width), when the visit started and ended, how long the game was on screen and being played, and how many questions were answered and how far the player got. It does not record the IP address, the exact device, location, or anything typed. Only the site administrator can see these records. They are used to find problems (for example a game that looks wrong on phones) and to see how the games are used, and they are deleted after 30 days.

From teachers. Teachers sign in with Google. The site saves their Google email address, their classes and class codes, the assignments and quizzes they create, and any messages they send to site support or post on the teacher board. A teacher who asks for access also gives their name and school.

What we never collect from students: email addresses, passwords, full last names, birthdays, photos, home addresses, phone numbers or location. Students never sign in with Google or any other account.

Microphone. A few ear training activities (singing a note, the tuner) can use the microphone, only after the browser asks for permission. The sound is analyzed on the student's own device while they play. It is never recorded, saved or sent anywhere.

How it is used

Student information is used only to run the games: to save progress, show leaderboards, let teachers see their students' practice, assignments and quiz grades, and (with the visit log) to keep the games working well. It is never sold, rented, shared for marketing, or used for ads. No student information is sent to AI services or used to train AI.

Where it is stored

  • Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore). Scores, progress, practice time, quiz results, the visit log, classes and teacher accounts are stored in a Firebase database run by Google on behalf of the site. Its security rules control who can read each kind of record.
  • The student's own browser. The name, class code, settings and some progress are also saved in the browser on the device the student uses, so they don't have to type them in every time. This stays on that device.
  • Moving between addresses. When the site moved to its new addresses, a small piece of code copies that saved browser information from the old address to the new one, inside the same browser. It travels in the part of the link after the # sign, which browsers never send to any server, and only between the site's own three addresses.
  • Hosting. The pages are served by Cloudflare and GitHub Pages, with a backup copy on Google Firebase Hosting. Some fonts and instrument sounds load from Google Fonts and free public sound libraries. Like any website, these services can see a visitor's IP address when a page or file loads, but they receive no student names or results.

Who can see it

WhoWhat they can see
The studentTheir own scores, progress and reports
The student's teacherResults, practice, progress, quiz grades and feedback notes for their own classes only
Other studentsLeaderboards (see below)
The site administrator (David Milnes)Everything, including the visit log, to run and support the site

Leaderboards. Class leaderboards show a first name, last initial and score, and only students in that class can see them. The World leaderboards show the top scores from every class with a first name and last initial, and anyone can see them.

Children under 13

The site is made for school use, and some students are under 13. It is designed to collect as little as possible from them: no email, no account, and only a first name and last initial. When a school or teacher uses the site with students, the school acts on behalf of parents in agreeing to this educational use, as the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) allows. The site does not ask children for more information than a game needs.

Deleting information

  • Teachers can delete their students' scores and progress, and whole classes, from the teacher dashboard.
  • A parent or student can ask the teacher, or write to privacy@musictheoryacademy.org, to have a student's information deleted. We will delete it within 30 days.
  • A teacher can ask to have their account and all of their classes deleted at any time.

How long we keep it

Visit log records are deleted after 30 days. Student results are kept only as long as they are useful for the class; we delete student records at the end of each school year.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. We will not start collecting new kinds of student information without updating this policy first.
